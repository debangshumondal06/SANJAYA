Civic Reporting Handbook

A practical resource to help residents write reports that municipal engineers can act on immediately, safely, and effectively.

1. Report-Writing Tips

When writing a description, you do not need formal bureaucratic terminology. Ordinary, concrete language is always most helpful for municipal supervisors.

The Three Helpful Elements:
  • What: What is physically damaged? (e.g. "Sunken paver", "Cracked concrete slab", "Overflowing organic food waste").
  • Scale: Approximate size (e.g. "About 1 meter wide, knee-deep", "Spilling onto two lanes of traffic").
  • Consequence: How does it impact people? (e.g. "Pedestrians forced into vehicle lane", "Two-wheelers losing balance").

Good example: "Deep pothole roughly 1.2m across on the northbound lane right outside the medical store. Water has filled it so motorcyclists cannot see the depth at night."

2. Evidence & Photography Guidelines

A single well-framed photograph is worth a dozen phone calls. It allows the dispatch engineer to verify whether they need manual shovels, cold asphalt, or heavy steamrollers before leaving the yard.

✓ Recommended: Step Back 2 Paces

Take a medium-distance photo showing both the defect and the street context (such as a shop sign, building gate, or road curb).

✕ Avoid: Extreme Close-ups

A photo showing only broken gravel with zero surroundings leaves the inspection officer wondering where on a 2-kilometer road the hole actually is.

Lighting Tip: If taking photos at dusk or night, try to frame a nearby streetlight or building light in the shot to improve contrast, or turn on your device camera flash.

3. Pinpointing Accurate Location

GPS satellites can sometimes drift by 20 to 50 meters in dense urban streets with tall apartment buildings. Always combine GPS coordinates with physical text anchors:

  • Street Pole Number: Most metal or concrete street lamps have a stenciled number (e.g., #LP-42). Mentioning this is foolproof.
  • Opposite Landmark: Name a permanent structure (bank, post office, temple, school gate) rather than a temporary roadside vendor.
  • Intersection Names: Mention cross-streets (e.g. "Southern Avenue near junction of Lake Temple Road").

4. Resident Safety Guidelines

⚠️ Critical Safety Rule Never risk physical injury or enter traffic flow to take a photo. A text description from the safe perimeter of a sidewalk is always sufficient.
  • Electrical Wires: If an electric cable or transformer is sparking or sagging near water, stay at least 10 meters away. Call 112 or the electric emergency utility immediately.
  • Flooded Gully Pits: Never step into opaque standing floodwater to investigate whether a manhole lid is displaced.
  • Heavy Traffic: Stand safely on the footpath or island when taking pictures.

5. Accessibility & Inclusivity

SANJAYA is engineered to comply with WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility standards. We believe every resident has an equal right to civic redress:

  • Voice Note Support: If typing is difficult or inconvenient on a mobile keyboard, simply tap the "Record Voice Note" button in the report flow.
  • Regional Languages: SANJAYA supports English, Hindi, and Bengali. Use the language selector at the top right anytime.
  • Keyboard & Screen Reader Accessible: All buttons, category selectors, and progress steps can be navigated purely via Tab, Enter, and Space keys.

6. Glossary of Status Terms

What does each milestone on your ticket timeline mean in real municipal terms?

Received Report Authenticated into Registry
Your report has entered the central intake pool. The location coordinates have been parsed and an initial fraud/spam check passed.
Being reviewed Jurisdiction & Duplicate Check
A regional coordinator checks whether the issue falls under municipal jurisdiction (vs state highway or railway land) and merges any existing reports from neighbors.
Sent to responsible team Work Order Dispatched
A formal job order has been placed into the specialized field unit's schedule (e.g. Asphalt Maintenance Team, Conservancy Compactor Crew).
Work in progress Crew Mobilized On Site
Barricades erected, excavation, desilting, or hot-mix asphalt application is actively underway during the assigned work shift.
Resolved Inspection & Case Archival
Post-remediation inspection logged by supervisor with photographic verification. Barricades cleared and case safely closed.
